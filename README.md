
# Marketing Funnel & Conversion Performance Analysis

## Project Overview
This project focused on analyzing a bank marketing campaign dataset to evaluate customer conversion behaviour, funnel performance, and campaign effectiveness using Microsoft Power BI.

The goal of the analysis was to identify customer drop-off points, understand which customer groups converted the most, and provide recommendations to improve future marketing campaigns.

## Tools Used
- Microsoft Power BI
- Power Query
- DAX
- Microsoft Excel

## Data Preparation
The dataset was cleaned and prepared in Excel before being imported into Power BI.

The following steps were performed:
- Split CSV data using semicolon delimiters
- Checked for missing values
- Removed exact duplicate records
- Verified data types
- Standardized categorical data
- Created helper columns for analysis

Helper columns created:
- Age Group
- Conversion Status
- Contact Frequency
- Campaign Outcome Group

## Key Findings & Insights

- The highest conversion levels occurred during March, September, October, and December. This may suggest that customer financial behaviour changes during certain times of the year, particularly around seasonal spending periods and end-of-year financial planning.

- Customers aged 30–40 recorded the strongest conversion levels. This age group is often financially active, career-focused, and more likely to engage with banking products such as savings and investment plans.

- Administrative workers converted more frequently than technicians and several other professions. This may indicate differences in financial stability, work environment, or accessibility to banking products and marketing communication.

- Married customers showed higher conversion levels compared to single customers. Married customers may be more financially focused and more likely to plan for long-term financial security.

- Customers with university degrees converted more frequently than customers with only high school education and others. This may suggest that customers with higher education levels respond more positively to financial marketing campaigns and understand banking products more clearly.

- Many customers converted after only one contact attempt, while customers contacted repeatedly showed lower conversion performance. This may indicate that excessive contact attempts reduce customer engagement and create customer fatigue.

- Customers with no previous campaign outcome recorded the highest conversion levels. This suggests that targeting new or previously uncontacted customers may create stronger conversion opportunities than repeatedly targeting customers from previous campaigns.

## Recommendations
- Focus campaigns on high-performing customer segments.
- Reduce excessive customer contact frequency.
- Improve campaign timing during high-performing months.
- Use customer segmentation for more targeted marketing strategies.
- Continue targeting new and previously uncontacted customers.
